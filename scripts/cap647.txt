here is a story.

i was 51. twenty two years adjusting hail claims. ladders, chalk, a ten by ten test square on every slope. i could tell a bruised shingle from a scuffed one with my thumb before most adjusters got their harness on.

then the carrier rolled out drone photos with a damage model scoring each roof. at the regional meeting, on the call recording, i said it: "a drone can photograph a roof. it can't feel a soft deck. you need a thumb on the shingle."

they ran it beside us for one storm season. every roof got my inspection and the model's score. then a second adjuster reinspected a random sample blind and called each one fresh.

on hail count we were close. it wasn't sharper than me. i was right about that part.

then the auditor split my calls by one field i never thought mattered: the notes line where i wrote how the visit went. friendly homeowner, coffee on the porch, kids waving at the ladder. or the guy who met me in the driveway yelling about his premium.

same damage, different answers. on the friendly roofs i approved full replacement more often than the blind reinspection did. on the hostile ones i went the other way. the model had no porch. it gave the same roof the same score every time.

i read that in my truck in a cul de sac with the ladder still on the rack and my face went hot. twenty two years i'd been proud of my thumb. nobody had ever checked my mood.

it didn't know more roofs than me. it just couldn't be talked into anything.

so i changed the job. the model scores every roof first and i only climb the ones it marks uncertain or the ones where i'd flip its call. when i disagree i write down why before i see the homeowner's name. and once a quarter i pull my own overrides and sort them by how the visit felt.

stop asking if the machine knows more than you. ask what it can't be talked into. then find out what you can.
