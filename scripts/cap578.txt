here is a story.

i was 41. fourteen years giving walking tours of the old town, two hours, same cobblestones, a red umbrella held up so nobody got lost. people think the job is dates and dead kings. it isn't. it's knowing where to stand. which corner makes the cathedral fill the whole sky. which alley smells like bread at ten am.

three years ago the city held a meeting about self guided audio apps. i stood up and said it on the record: an app can read you a date. it can't tell you where to stand. somebody filmed it. the tourism office put the clip on their page for a week.

then one wet tuesday in april, twenty two people started my tour. around the second square i noticed earbuds. one couple drifted left down a lane i never use. then a family. then four more. an ai guide was talking in their ears, following their phones street by street, answering whatever they asked, in their own language. by the bell tower i had nine people. i counted twice.

the rain got into my umbrella and dripped on my collar. i finished the story about the fire of 1680 to nine polite faces, and i could hear my own voice the way you hear a radio in an empty shop.

that night i downloaded the app and walked my own route with it. it was good. it stood me in the right corners. it knew things i'd forgotten. what it couldn't do was knock on the baker's back door, or get us into the courtyard the old woman only opens for people she recognizes.

so my tour changed. i tell people to listen to the app the night before. i don't recite dates anymore. i ask what they already heard, then i take them through the doors that only open for a face the neighborhood knows. smaller groups. longer stops. more questions than answers.

the lesson: the app knows every street. it doesn't know which doors open for you.

stop reciting the street. start opening the doors.
