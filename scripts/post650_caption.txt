here is a story.

i was 44. sixteen years dating timber by its tree rings. a core from a barn beam, a panel from behind a painting, a cello back. i'd measure the rings to a hundredth of a millimeter and slide the sequence along a master chronology until it locked. the good matches you can feel. the wood tells you the year.

then the lab licensed a matching model. at the methods meeting, in the minutes, i said: "it will find the statistics. it will not read the wood. on short sequences, missing rings, false rings, it will date confidently and date wrong."

so they ran us blind for a season. every sample got a date from me and a date from the model. plus one extra column i proposed myself: how sure are you. i wrote a number on every call.

on dates, we tied. i did not expect that. i could have lived with that.

the confidence column was the part i couldn't live with. when the model said 95, it was right 19 times in 20. when i wrote certain, i was right 4 times in 5. and my certain calls clustered exactly where i'd warned about: short sequences, under sixty rings. the samples with the least data were the ones i felt surest about. the model had marked most of those undated.

the director put both curves on one slide. mine bent the wrong way.

i stood in the sample store, where it's kept cold, for a long time. sixteen years of being about as right as a machine, and sixteen years of not knowing which of my own calls to doubt. the wood never told me the year. it told me a story, and i had learned to believe stories.

now the model dates everything. anything it flags under threshold comes to me. anything i mark certain with under sixty rings goes back for a second run. the lab went from four of us to me. i'm the appeals desk for my own old weakness.

you don't lose to the machine on being right. you lose on knowing when you're wrong.

stop tracking your hit rate. start tracking what your "certain" is worth.
